Saying the words right is half of it. These are the small habits that make people answer you warmly.

THE ONE TO REMEMBER

Right hand,
always.

Hand over money, food, or a gift with your right hand — or with both. The left hand is reserved for the bathroom, so using it to give something reads as rude, even from a visitor who means well.

ALL TIPS

Small habits, big DIFFERENCE

8 Tips

Visiting

Shoes off indors

Look at the doorway. If there are sandals outside a home or a prayer room, yours come off too.

Eating

Wait for the host's cue

At a shared meal, let the oldest person or host take food first. Once they say "Silakan," dig in.

Speaking

Soften it with “ya”

Adding ya at the end turns an order into a request. “Nggak pedas, ya” lands much softer than “nggak pedas”.

Greeting

Use Bapak and Ibu

For anyone older, Pak or Bu is safer than a name. It is polite without being stiff.

Time

“Jam karet” is real

Social plans can run late. For an appointment, confirm the time the day before.

Speaking

Point with your thumb

Eating

Spoon in right, fork in left

Most local dishes are eaten with a spoon, using the fork to push food onto it. Knives rarely appear at the table.

Money

Small notes save time

Many stalls cannot break a large note early in the day. Keep 2.000 and 5.000 notes for drinks and parking.

QUICK LIST

Before you walk IN

DO THIS

  • Greet with “Permisi” before asking anything

  • Take and give with your right hand

  • Ask before taking someone’s photo

  • Accept tea or snacks, even a sip

SKIP THIS

  • Pointing with your index finger

  • Bargaining hard at a fixed-price shop

  • Putting your feet up toward people

  • Raising your voice to be understood
